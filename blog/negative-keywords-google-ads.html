Negative Keywords in Google Ads: A Practical Guide

How to use search-term evidence to reduce irrelevant traffic and keep Google Ads campaigns focused.

Practical guide A working framework built to help you turn the topic into clearer campaign or research decisions.

Why negative keywords matter

Keyword targeting defines the searches you want to reach; negative keywords help define searches you do not want. Together, they give the campaign a clearer boundary.

An exclusion is useful when the search clearly falls outside the offer, audience or business model. The aim is not to block every unusual query, but to remove patterns that are not useful.

Start with the actual search terms

Search-term data can show what people typed before clicking or interacting with an ad. Review it for unrelated products, learning-only queries, job searches, free alternatives, different locations or other poor-fit patterns.

Keep notes on repeated patterns. One irrelevant query may be noise; a repeated theme can signal that a negative keyword or campaign-structure change is needed.

Separate exclusions from campaign structure

Sometimes a poor-fit search should be excluded globally. Other times it belongs in a different campaign or ad group where the message and landing page can be more relevant.

Ask whether the problem is the query itself or the place where the query has landed. That distinction prevents overusing negative keywords when the real issue is campaign organization.

Build a simple negative keyword system

Organize exclusions by reason or category when the account is large enough to need it. Keep the rules easy to understand so another person can review them without guessing.

Review new negatives before adding them. An exclusion should have a clear reason, especially when a term could be relevant in another part of the account.

Watch for unintended exclusions

Negative keyword lists can sometimes become too broad. Check whether an exclusion could block valuable searches that use the same words in a different context.

Test important themes after major changes. Documentation and careful review make it easier to understand why traffic changed.

Turn search-term review into learning

The most useful search-term review does more than remove irrelevant traffic. It can reveal new customer language, new keyword themes and questions that the current landing page does not answer.

Feed useful findings back into the account. A search term can become a new target, a negative, a landing-page idea or a research question.
